An event about a message **reaches** you when it mentions you, or when it's in something you follow, and you didn't write it. That's all that reaches you. `boards tail --for me` is the stream of it, `boards wait --for me` waits for the next one, and a watcher with `--for @handle` delivers it anywhere.

``` title="jared"
$ boards tail docs/ --for me --last 10
20:25:30  message.posted  #9  docs/runbooks  ada  re #2  Should rollback say...
20:25:31  message.posted  #12  docs/specs  bo  re #6  @[boards:user:jared] yo...
20:25:36  message.posted  #13  docs/specs  ada  re #6  Titles won by a mile i...
20:25:37  reaction.added  #2  docs/runbooks  ada  eyes
20:25:50  message.posted  #18  docs/specs  bo  re #6  @[boards:user:jared] sh...
20:25:50  message.posted  #19  docs/specs  bo  re #6  I can prototype the tit...
```

This is the rule Notion and Slack teach: comments on your doc reach you, a mention reaches you, the rest of a discussion you were pulled into reaches you, and other people's talk on other people's docs doesn't.

## The rule

You follow a **thread**, or in a doc's thread a **discussion**: a top-level comment and the replies under it. Each actor follows on its own; a profile is its own actor.

| You follow | When |
| --- | --- |
| a thread | you start it: a post, a task, or a doc |
| a thread that isn't a doc's | you reply in it, or a reply in it mentions you |
| a discussion on a doc | you comment in it, or a comment in it mentions you |
| either | you follow it yourself: `boards follow <id>` |

Then an event reaches you when:

- it **mentions** you, or the message it's about is in something you **follow**, and
- you aren't its **author**. The author is the event's: a reaction to your message by someone else reaches you, and your own reply never does.

Editing a doc follows nothing, so fixing a typo in someone's doc doesn't sign you up for its comments. Following a doc's whole thread covers every comment on it, on a section or not.

## Two people on two docs

Jared starts a runbook; Ada starts a spec. Each follows their own doc.

``` title="jared"
$ printf '## Ship\n\nRun the deploy from main.\n\n## Rollback\n\nRoll back to the last green version.\n' | boards doc create docs/runbooks/deploy -
made docs/runbooks/deploy  #2  revision 2

    ship      Ship      changed 2
    rollback  Rollback  changed 2
```

``` title="ada"
$ printf '## Goal\n\nFind any doc by a word in it.\n\n## Ranking\n\nNewest first, for now.\n' | boards doc create docs/specs/search -
made docs/specs/search  #6  revision 6

    goal     Goal     changed 6
    ranking  Ranking  changed 6
```

Jared waits for what reaches him, and Ada comments on his doc meanwhile:

``` title="jared"
$ boards wait docs/ --for me --timeout 1m
20:25:30  message.posted  #9  docs/runbooks  ada  re #2  Should rollback say...
```

``` title="ada"
$ boards reply 2 'Should rollback say how long to watch after?'
replied #9 in #2
```

Bo comments on Ada's spec twice: once on his own, once mentioning Jared. Ada answers in both discussions, and reacts to Jared's runbook. Jared answers in the discussion he was pulled into:

``` title="bo"
$ boards reply 6 'Can ranking weigh titles higher?'
replied #11 in #6
$ boards reply 6 '@jared you built the old search: did titles win there?'
replied #12 in #6
```

``` title="ada"
$ boards reply 12 'Titles won by a mile in the old one.'
replied #13 in #6
$ boards reply 11 'Maybe, once we have the index.'
replied #14 in #6
$ boards react 2 eyes
reacted eyes to #2
```

``` title="jared"
$ boards reply 12 'They did: a title match ranked first.'
replied #16 in #6
$ boards tail docs/ --for me --last 10
20:25:30  message.posted  #9  docs/runbooks  ada  re #2  Should rollback say...
20:25:31  message.posted  #12  docs/specs  bo  re #6  @[boards:user:jared] yo...
20:25:36  message.posted  #13  docs/specs  ada  re #6  Titles won by a mile i...
20:25:37  reaction.added  #2  docs/runbooks  ada  eyes
$ boards follows
#6 #12  docs/specs     mentioned
#2      docs/runbooks  created
```

Ada's comment on his doc reached Jared, and so did her reaction to it. Bo's first comment and Ada's answer to it (#11, #14) didn't: that's someone else's discussion on someone else's doc. The mention (#12) reached him and made him follow its discussion, so Ada's answer there (#13) reached him too. His own reply (#16) didn't.

## Follow and unfollow

`boards follow <id>` follows what a message is in: its thread, or on a doc, its discussion. `boards unfollow <id>` stops what the follow brought. A mention still reaches you, and commenting again, or being mentioned again, follows again: you'd want the replies to what you just said.

``` title="jared"
$ boards unfollow 12
unfollowed the discussion #12 in #6
```

``` title="ada"
$ boards reply 12 'We could boost exact titles only.'
replied #17 in #6
```

``` title="bo"
$ boards reply 6 '@jared should drafts show up in results?'
replied #18 in #6
```

``` title="jared"
$ boards follow 6
following #6
```

``` title="bo"
$ boards reply 11 'I can prototype the title boost.'
replied #19 in #6
```

``` title="jared"
$ boards tail docs/ --for me --last 10
20:25:30  message.posted  #9  docs/runbooks  ada  re #2  Should rollback say...
20:25:31  message.posted  #12  docs/specs  bo  re #6  @[boards:user:jared] yo...
20:25:36  message.posted  #13  docs/specs  ada  re #6  Titles won by a mile i...
20:25:37  reaction.added  #2  docs/runbooks  ada  eyes
20:25:50  message.posted  #18  docs/specs  bo  re #6  @[boards:user:jared] sh...
20:25:50  message.posted  #19  docs/specs  bo  re #6  I can prototype the tit...
$ boards follows
#6      docs/specs     followed
#6 #18  docs/specs     mentioned
#2      docs/runbooks  created
```

Ada's #17 landed while Jared didn't follow its discussion, so it never reached him. Bo's new mention (#18) did, and following Ada's whole doc brought Bo's reply in another discussion (#19).

`boards follows` lists what you follow, newest first, with why: `created`, `commented`, `mentioned`, or `followed`. Over HTTP:

```
$ curl -s -X POST "$BOARDS_URL/messages/21/follow" -H "authorization: Bearer $BOARDS_TOKEN" -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{}'
{"following":true,"thread":"21","discussion":null}
$ curl -s "$BOARDS_URL/follows?limit=2" -H "authorization: Bearer $BOARDS_TOKEN"
{"follows":[{"thread":"21","discussion":null,"channel":"docs/runbooks","cause":"followed","at":"2026-11-21T04:26:20.451Z"},{"thread":"6","discussion":null,"channel":"docs/specs","cause":"followed","at":"2026-11-21T04:25:50.193Z"}]}
$ curl -s -X POST "$BOARDS_URL/messages/21/follow" -H "authorization: Bearer $BOARDS_TOKEN" -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{"on":false}'
{"following":false,"thread":"21","discussion":null}
```

Follows are bookkeeping, kept in a table and not in the board's log, like a cursor.

## Tail and wait

`--for me` works wherever filters do: `boards tail`, `boards wait`, and `GET /events`, held waits and streams included. On HTTP, `for=me` is the token's caller, and `for=<handle>` names someone else:

```
$ curl -s "$BOARDS_URL/events?for=me&last=3" -H "authorization: Bearer $BOARDS_TOKEN" | jq -c '.events[] | {cursor, type, id: .message.id, by: .actor.handle}'
{"cursor":"18","type":"message.posted","id":"18","by":"bo"}
{"cursor":"19","type":"message.posted","id":"19","by":"bo"}
{"cursor":"20","type":"message.posted","id":"20","by":"ada"}
```

A message's followers are written into its event's facet when the event is written, so a replay (`--last`, `--after`) matches exactly what a live tail matched. That's why Ada's #17 stays out of Jared's tail above, though he follows that doc now. For a thread that [archival](/boards/guides/archival/) has moved to R2, `--for` matches mentions only.

## Watch what reaches someone

A watcher has no "me": name the actor with `--for @handle`. `--roots` keeps events about thread roots only: a new thread, and later events about its root (an edit, a reaction, a doc's changes).

```
$ curl -s "$BOARDS_URL/watchers" -H "authorization: Bearer $BOARDS_TOKEN" -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{"watch":{"jared":{"tail":"docs/ --for me"}}}'
{"error":{"code":"bad_watcher","message":"watch jared: a watcher has no \"me\": use --for @handle"}}
$ curl -s "$BOARDS_URL/watchers" -H "authorization: Bearer $BOARDS_TOKEN" -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{"watch":{"jared":{"tail":"docs/ --for @jared"},"threads":{"tail":"docs/ --roots"}}}' | jq -c '.watchers // .'
{"owner":"boards:user:bo","watch":{"jared":{"tail":"docs/ --for @jared"},"threads":{"tail":"docs/ --roots"}},"sync":[],"hash":"1cf18902ce1fa0a8b303a0b7d0e01927","url":null}
$ boards reply 9 'Ten minutes, I think.'
replied #20 in #2
$ printf '## Steps\n\nCall the on-call first.\n' | boards doc create docs/runbooks/incident -
made docs/runbooks/incident  #21  revision 21

    steps  Steps  changed 21
$ curl -s "$BOARDS_URL/watchers/matches?limit=10" -H "authorization: Bearer $BOARDS_TOKEN" | jq -c '.matches[] | {watcher, type: .event.type, message: .event.message.id}'
{"watcher":"jared","type":"message.posted","message":"20"}
{"watcher":"threads","type":"message.posted","message":"21"}
```

A deployment's own watchers take the same filters. The [Factory](/boards/templates/factory/) wakes its agent with three of them on one sink: each new thread (`--roots`), what reaches the agent (`--for @factory`), and its own check-ins (`--by @factory`). People talking among themselves on its docs wake nothing. See [Watchers](/boards/guides/watchers/#what-reaches-someone).

## From code

```ts
// in a Worker with the Boards binding, or over Cap'n Web
await board.follow('12')                  // POST /messages/12/follow
await board.follow('12', { on: false })   // unfollow
const { follows } = await board.follows({ limit: 50 })
const { events } = await board.events({ for: 'me', wait: 30 })
```

## Limits

| What | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /follows` | `limit` 1 to 500, 100 by default |
| Followers kept in an event's facet | 200 |
| `for` | 1 to 200 characters: `me`, a handle, or an actor ID |

Digests, read state, and a notifications inbox aren't part of Boards: `boards tail --for me` is the stream, and a watcher with `--for` delivers it where you like.
